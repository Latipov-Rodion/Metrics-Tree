# ROAS vs ROMI — how to calculate both and what ROAS you need to break even

"Our ROAS is 350%, ads are paying off" is one of the most expensive sentences in marketing. ROAS shows revenue per dollar of ad spend, but says nothing about profit. A store running 3.5x ROAS may be making money on every campaign — or losing it. Margin decides. ROMI answers the question the owner actually cares about: how much profit did marketing generate?

## Formulas

**ROAS (Return on Ad Spend) = Revenue from ads / Ad spend**

Expressed as a multiplier (3.5x) or a percentage (350%). Calculated per campaign, channel or ad.

**ROMI (Return on Marketing Investment) = (Gross profit from marketing − Marketing cost) / Marketing cost × 100%**

Gross profit = revenue × margin (ideally contribution margin: after COGS, shipping, payment fees and returns).

The key differences:

| Parameter | ROAS | ROMI |
|--|------|------|
| Numerator | Revenue | Profit minus cost |
| Denominator | Ad budget only | All marketing cost (ads, agency, creative, tools) |
| Break-even point | Depends on margin | Always 0% |
| Used for | Optimizing campaigns and bids | Deciding whether to invest in marketing at all |

## Worked example

An online store, one month:
- Ad spend: **$20,000**
- Attributed revenue: **$70,000**
- Contribution margin: **35%**

ROAS = 70,000 / 20,000 = **3.5x**

Profit from sales = 70,000 × 0.35 = $24,500
ROMI = (24,500 − 20,000) / 20,000 = **22.5%**

Marketing pays off: every ad dollar came back and brought another 22.5 cents of profit.

Now the same 3.5x ROAS, but with a **25%** margin (say, a discount-heavy sale):

Profit = 70,000 × 0.25 = $17,500
ROMI = (17,500 − 20,000) / 20,000 = **−12.5%**

ROAS didn't move, yet the campaign now loses money. That's why ROAS without margin is a half-truth.

## Break-even ROAS: the one formula every marketer needs

**Break-even ROAS = 1 / Margin**

| Contribution margin | Break-even ROAS |
|---------------------|-----------------|
| 20% | 5.0x |
| 25% | 4.0x |
| 35% | 2.86x |
| 50% | 2.0x |
| 70% | 1.43x |

Calculate this number once for your product mix and keep it in view. Anything below it means you're buying revenue at a loss. Work out your margin with the [/en/contributionMargin](/en/contributionMargin) calculator.

## ROAS benchmarks by industry

The industry thresholds used by the MetricTree [/en/roas](/en/roas) calculator:

| Industry | Poor | Normal | Good |
|----------|------|--------|------|
| **Universal** | <2x | 2–4x | >4x |
| **E-commerce** | <2x | 2–5x | >5x |
| **SaaS** | <1.5x | 1.5–4x | >4x |
| **Mobile apps** | <1x | 1–2.5x | >2.5x |
| **Media** | <1.2x | 1.2–3x | >3x |

Why are SaaS and mobile thresholds lower? Because ROAS there is usually measured on first-period revenue, while customers pay for months. A 1.5x first-month ROAS on a subscription can be an excellent campaign if the customer stays two years. For these models look at CAC payback too — [/en/cacPayback](/en/cacPayback).

In our example, 3.5x is normal for e-commerce, and at a 35% margin the campaign is profitable. At a 25% margin the same "normal" number loses money: the break-even threshold is 4x.

## MER — an honesty check for ROAS

Ad platforms tend to claim credit: overlapping attribution, view-through conversions, buyers who would have purchased anyway. The sum of ROAS across all ad accounts is often larger than your real revenue.

The check is **MER (Marketing Efficiency Ratio) = Total revenue / Total marketing spend**. DTC guideline: <2 weak, 2–3 normal, 3–5 good, >5 excellent. If platform ROAS keeps rising while MER stays flat, attribution is fooling you. Calculator — [/en/mer](/en/mer).

## ROMI with LTV

For businesses with repeat purchases or subscriptions, ROMI on the first order understates the effect. A fairer version:

**ROMI (LTV) = (LTV × margin × new customers − Marketing cost) / Marketing cost**

But be careful: LTV is a forecast. Use a conservative horizon (for example, 12 months) and validate it against real cohorts. Otherwise it's easy to "prove" that anything pays off.

## Common mistakes

### 1. Calculating ROMI on revenue instead of profit
"ROMI = (revenue − cost) / cost" is just ROAS minus one, not ROMI. With that formula a loss-making campaign looks profitable.

### 2. Ignoring indirect costs
Agency fees, creative production, tools, the performance team's salaries. They're not in ROAS, but they belong in ROMI.

### 3. Trusting platform attribution 100%
Last-click and view-through inflate the numbers. Cross-check with MER and, where possible, incrementality tests (switching a channel off in some regions).

### 4. One margin for the whole catalog
If ads mostly sell low-margin products, the store's average margin overstates ROMI. Use the margin of the products the campaign actually sold.

### 5. Comparing ROAS across business models
2x in a mobile game and 2x in an electronics store are completely different results. Compare against your industry's norm and your own break-even threshold.

### 6. Optimizing for ROAS alone
The highest ROAS almost always comes at the smallest budget (warm audiences, branded search). The goal is maximum profit, not the maximum ratio.

## FAQ

### What is a good ROAS?
One that sits above your break-even threshold (1 / margin) with room for indirect costs. As an industry guideline: 2–5x is normal for e-commerce and above 5x is good; 1.5–4x is normal for SaaS.

### Does 100% ROAS mean the ads paid off?
No. A 1x (100%) ROAS means revenue equals ad spend. But that revenue still has to cover COGS, shipping and fees — so the campaign loses money at almost any margin.

### Can I calculate ROMI for SEO and content?
Yes, with the same formula: writers' salaries, tools and contractors go in the denominator; profit from organic customers goes in the numerator. Use a longer horizon for SEO — 6–12 months or more.

### How is ROAS different from MER and CAC?
ROAS measures the efficiency of a specific campaign or channel based on attribution data. MER measures the efficiency of all marketing against the company's real revenue, with no attribution. CAC answers a different question: what one new customer costs, not how much revenue an ad dollar brought in. In e-commerce it's worth keeping all three on one dashboard.

## Bottom line

ROAS is an operating metric for optimizing campaigns. ROMI is a financial metric for deciding how much to invest in marketing. Margin connects them: know your break-even ROAS, check ad platforms against MER, and calculate ROMI on profit, not revenue.
