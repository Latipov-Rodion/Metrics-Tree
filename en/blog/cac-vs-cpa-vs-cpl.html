CAC vs CPA vs CPL — what's the difference and which one to optimize

"CPL is down by half, marketing is crushing it" — and two months later there are fewer customers and CAC has gone up. That happens when three similar-sounding cost metrics get mixed up. CPL, CPA and CAC measure different points of the funnel, and optimizing one can easily make another worse.

Definitions and formulas

CPL (Cost per Lead) = Channel spend / Number of leads

A lead is a contact who left their details: a form, a request, a newsletter sign-up, a content download. CPL answers "how much does it cost to get someone into the funnel?"

CPA (Cost per Action, or Cost per Acquisition) = Spend / Number of target actions

You define the target action: sign-up, trial, demo, first purchase, install. CPA is the most flexible of the three, which is why it always needs a qualifier: CPA of what, exactly.

CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost) = Total acquisition cost / Number of new paying customers

The numerator includes not just ads but marketing and sales salaries, agencies, tools and creative. The denominator counts only people who paid. CAC is the only one of the three you can compare directly with LTV.

All three are linked through conversion:

CAC (media only) = CPL / Lead-to-customer conversion rate

Worked example: one funnel, three numbers

A B2B SaaS company, one month:

  • Ad spend: $20,000
  • SDR and marketing salaries, tools: $16,000 (total acquisition cost $36,000)
  • Leads (inbound requests): 400
  • Demos held (target action): 80
  • New paying customers: 24

CPL = 20,000 / 400 = $50
CPA (demo) = 20,000 / 80 = $250
CAC (media only) = 20,000 / 24 = $833
CAC (fully loaded) = 36,000 / 24 = $1,500

Check via conversion: lead → customer = 24 / 400 = 6%. $50 / 0.06 = $833 ✓

The same funnel produces numbers from $50 to $1,500. They're all correct — they just answer different questions.

How they compare with MetricTree's SaaS thresholds:

  • CPL of $50 — within the $20–$200 per MQL guideline.
  • CPA of $250 per demo — above the $30–$150 guideline for trials/demos. The bottleneck is lead-to-demo conversion (20%).
  • In SaaS, CAC is judged by payback. With $150/month ARPA and 80% gross margin, CAC payback = 1,500 / (150 × 0.8) ≈ 12.5 months — normal (6–18 months), but not excellent (<6 months).

Why a cheap CPL is not a cheap customer

Add a second channel: a lead magnet with a downloadable checklist. Same $20,000 budget, but now there are 1,000 leads — CPL is only $20. However, only 1.5% convert to customers, which is 15 customers.

When to use which

  • CPL — compare creatives, placements and offers within one channel when payment is still far away (long B2B cycles).
  • CPA — set up automated bidding in an ad platform. Pick an action as close to payment as possible: "paid trial" beats "sign-up".
  • CAC — decide how much you can spend on acquisition at all, compare channels with each other, and calculate LTV:CAC and payback.

Common mistakes

1. Calling CPA "CAC"

If the denominator is sign-ups rather than payments, it's CPA. You can't compare it with LTV: you'd be dividing a customer's value by the cost of a person who may never pay.

2. Counting only ad spend in CAC

In B2B, sales and marketing salaries often exceed the media budget. Media-only CAC is useful for comparing channels, but unit economics needs the fully loaded number.

3. Paying bonuses on CPL

The team will quickly learn to buy cheap, useless leads. Add lead-to-deal conversion to the KPI, or use CAC directly.

4. Ignoring the time lag

March leads become customers in May. Dividing March spend by March customers mixes different cohorts. For long sales cycles, calculate CAC by lead cohort.

5. Mixing new and returning customers

Reactivated customers and upsells aren't "new". Including them understates CAC.

FAQ

Are CPA and CAC the same thing?

Only if the target action is the first payment and the numerator includes all acquisition costs. Otherwise CPA is lower than CAC.

What is a good CPL?

One at which the resulting CAC, after all conversions, pays back. For B2B SaaS the guideline is $20–$200 per MQL, but judging CPL without lead-to-customer conversion is meaningless.

Bottom line

CPL and CPA are operating metrics for tuning ads. CAC is a financial metric for deciding how much to spend on growth. The link between them always runs through conversion: when you push CPL down, check that CAC isn't going up.
